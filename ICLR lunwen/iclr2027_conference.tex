\begin{abstract}
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) typically retrieves independently indexed chunks without modeling the structural relationships within documents. Consequently, it may retrieve relevant fragments without assembling the coherent, multi-granularity evidence required for complex questions. CascHyper-RAG is a multi-granularity hypergraph framework for knowledge question answering. The framework represents nested topic--chunk, chunk--sentence, and sentence--entity relations. It constructs an asymmetric bidirectional index with local attention fusion and applies query-side cascaded multi-granularity enhancement for retrieval and generation. The framework is evaluated against LLM-only and retrieval-based baselines on eight document QA datasets, including five machine-tool and three general-domain datasets, under five LLM backbones and three evidence-depth stages. CascHyper-RAG achieves the highest average score in every evaluated domain and backbone setting. On Mechanical QA, the framework improves over the strongest competing baseline by 1.1--2.9 points across LLMs and 2.6--2.9 points across evidence-depth stages, while retaining retrieval latency comparable to that of the strongest structured-retrieval baseline. It also increases full-chain evidence recall from 53.8\% to 74.8\%. This design links document-level scope to fine-grained evidence, helping the retriever assemble coherent support for knowledge-intensive answers. Code is available at \url{https://github.com/jia1223/CascHyper-rag}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Large language models (LLMs) offer a general interface for knowledge-intensive question answering. Yet, knowledge stored only in model parameters is incomplete, difficult to update, and can produce unsupported answers. Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) addresses this limitation by retrieving external evidence before generation \citep{Lewis2020RAG,Borgeaud2022RETRO,Asai2024SelfRAG}. Unlike parametric updates, retrieval can expose an answer's source and refresh supporting evidence without modifying the generator. These properties make RAG a practical foundation for systems that answer over evolving or specialized corpora. Its non-parametric knowledge source is especially useful in technical and other domain-specific settings. There, answers depend on specialized terminology, document-specific constraints, and evidence that may change over time \citep{Yang2025KBSLLMKB}. However, retrieval helps only when the system selects evidence that is both relevant and coherent for the question.

Most RAG systems independently index chunks and retrieve them by semantic similarity \citep{Lewis2020RAG,Karpukhin2020DPR,Devlin2019BERT,Izacard2023Atlas}. This supports knowledge updates without generator retraining and questions answerable from one passage \citep{Rajpurkar2016SQuAD,Kwiatkowski2019NaturalQuestions}, but relevant fragments need not form a coherent evidence path \citep{Liu2024LostMiddle}. Graph-based RAG organizes entities, relations, or communities \citep{Peng2025GraphRAGSurvey,Edge2025GraphRAG,Jin2024LLMGraphs,Zhong2023KGConstruction,Xu2024KGRAGCSQA,Blondel2008Louvain}, connecting distributed support through community summaries and entity paths \citep{Adjali2024MultiLevelRAG,Lan2023ComplexKBQA}. These structures do not explicitly preserve how topics delimit chunks, chunks contextualize sentences, and sentences ground entities. Hypergraphs represent high-order relations \citep{Feng2019HGNN,Gao2023HGNNPlus,Chien2022AllSet,Wu2021GNNSurvey}, but existing hypergraph RAG methods primarily organize entity-level or other single-level relations \citep{Feng2026HyperRAG}.

The resulting challenge is to retrieve evidence at several granularities without losing either contextual scope or factual specificity. For a complex question, topic-level information should restrict the search region, while sentence- and entity-level evidence should verify the final claim. Conversely, fine-grained evidence must remain interpretable in its document context. Queries are usually short and underspecified, whereas documents contain the hierarchy needed to contextualize their terms. The query representation must therefore be aligned with the same levels that organize the document index. A retrieval representation should connect these levels throughout the RAG pipeline, rather than treating them as independent signals.

To address this gap, we design CascHyper-RAG, a cascaded multi-granularity hypergraph framework for knowledge question answering. It constructs nested topic--chunk, chunk--sentence, and sentence--entity hypergraphs to combine document scope, contextual statements, and factual anchors. Its asymmetric bidirectional index propagates higher-level context downward and aggregates lower-level factual support upward. Query-side cascaded enhancement then uses these representations for topic-scoped chunk recall, sentence reranking, and multi-hop evidence retrieval. Thus, CascHyper-RAG models document hierarchy together with high-order relations throughout the retrieval pipeline.

Our contributions are as follows:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1em,labelsep=0.45em,topsep=0pt,partopsep=0pt,itemsep=0pt,parsep=0pt]
    \item \textbf{Cascaded multi-granularity hypergraph construction.} We model document evidence with nested topic--chunk, chunk--sentence, and sentence--entity hypergraphs, linking global scope to contextual and factual units.
    \item \textbf{Asymmetric bidirectional indexing with local attention fusion.} We combine bottom-up factual aggregation with top-down contextual propagation, enriching each level without replacing its original evidence signal.
    \item \textbf{Query-side cascaded multi-granularity enhancement.} We align queries with the document index to support topic-scoped chunk recall, multi-granularity reranking, shared-entity multi-hop retrieval.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related-work}

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) couples a retriever with a language model to provide non-parametric evidence for knowledge-intensive tasks \citep{Lewis2020RAG,Guu2020REALM,Borgeaud2022RETRO,Asai2024SelfRAG,Gao2024RAGSurvey,Huang2026RAGSurvey}. Dense retrieval improves evidence access through learned query and passage representations \citep{Karpukhin2020DPR,Devlin2019BERT,Izacard2023Atlas}. Query rewriting and hypothetical-document expansion further improve underspecified retrieval inputs \citep{Ma2023QueryRewriting,Gao2023HyDE}. Question-answering benchmarks and evaluation frameworks assess retrieval-augmented systems under varying evidence and generation conditions \citep{Rajpurkar2016SQuAD,Kwiatkowski2019NaturalQuestions,Es2024RAGAS,Fabbri2021SummEval,Zheng2023LLMJudge}. However, conventional RAG usually indexes and retrieves passages independently, limiting its ability to coordinate document scope with local factual evidence \citep{Adjali2024MultiLevelRAG,Lan2023ComplexKBQA,Liu2024LostMiddle}.

Graph-based RAG introduces entities, triples, communities, or knowledge graphs to connect related evidence \citep{Pan2024LLMKG,Peng2025GraphRAGSurvey,Jin2024LLMGraphs,Zhong2023KGConstruction,Xu2024KGRAGCSQA}. GraphRAG uses entity graphs and community reports for local and global query-focused summarization \citep{Edge2025GraphRAG}. LightRAG combines graph-based evidence with dual-level retrieval \citep{Guo2025LightRAG}. Graph neural encoders and entity-linking methods provide complementary mechanisms for aggregating neighborhoods and resolving entity mentions \citep{Kipf2017GCN,Hamilton2017GraphSAGE,Velickovic2018GAT,Wu2021GNNSurvey,Hoffart2011AIDA,Sevgili2022EntityLinkingSurvey,Yamada2020LUKE}. These methods extend retrieval beyond adjacent chunks, but they primarily organize pairwise relations, entity neighborhoods, or coarse summaries. They do not explicitly preserve the nested document hierarchy that determines how context should interpret local facts.

Hypergraphs generalize ordinary graphs by allowing one hyperedge to connect multiple vertices. Hypergraph neural networks can therefore represent high-order dependencies beyond independent pairwise links \citep{Feng2019HGNN,Gao2023HGNNPlus,Chien2022AllSet}. Hyper-RAG applies this principle to retrieval-augmented generation through multi-entity hypergraph organization \citep{Feng2026HyperRAG}. Existing hypergraph RAG methods, however, primarily construct hyperedges over entities or other single-level units. They do not model the document cascade from topics to chunks, sentences, and entities during both indexing and retrieval.Therefore, we propose CascHyper-RAG, which explicitly models this hierarchical document cascade to enable coordinated multi-granularity evidence retrieval.

\section{Method}\label{sec:method}

Given a document corpus, CascHyper-RAG retrieves evidence for a knowledge-intensive question by preserving relationships across four semantic levels: latent topics, chunks, sentences, and sentence-level entities. Figure~\ref{fig:holohyper_overall_framework} summarizes the pipeline. Offline indexing converts documents into a cascaded multi-granularity hypergraph and builds context-aware representations. At query time, the system constructs aligned query representations, retrieves evidence from coarse to fine granularity, expands through shared entities when needed, and supplies the selected context to the generator.

\subsection{Cascaded Multi-granularity Hypergraph Construction}

Let $\mathbf{T}$, $\mathbf{C}$, $\mathbf{S}$, and $\mathbf{E}$ denote the sets of topic anchors, chunks, sentences, and entities. A hyperedge can connect more than two vertices \citep{Zhou2006Hypergraphs}. We use this property to represent document organization as three nested sub-hypergraphs, rather than treating all evidence as a flat collection of passages. Appendix Figure~\ref{fig:hypergraph_construction} illustrates the resulting cascade.

In the topic--chunk hypergraph, chunks are vertices and latent topics are hyperedges. We construct a semantic-similarity graph over chunk representations and apply Leiden clustering to obtain topic anchors \citep{Traag2019Leiden}. Because a chunk can be associated with multiple topic hyperedges, we assign its memberships according to similarity with each topic anchor. This produces fuzzy topics that preserve associations between semantically related but physically distant document regions. The resulting membership weights determine the topic context used in index construction. Appendix~\ref{app:latent_topic_construction} specifies the similarity graph, topic anchors, and fuzzy-membership rule.

In the chunk--sentence hypergraph, chunks are hyperedges over their sentence vertices. A sentence-level unit may contain consecutive natural sentences when they express one complete idea; the corresponding extraction prompt is given in Appendix~\ref{app:sentence_extraction_prompt}. Thus, this level retains the physical boundary and local context of each source chunk.

In the sentence--entity hypergraph, sentences become hyperedges over extracted entities, actions, states, and other atomic semantic units. Each entity receives an importance weight from the extraction procedure; the entity-extraction prompt is given in Appendix~\ref{app:entity_extraction_prompt}.

\subsection{Asymmetric Bidirectional Cascaded Index}

\subsubsection{Local attention fusion}

Each semantic unit has a normalized raw embedding. To combine a target embedding $\mathbf{q}$ with a local feature set $K=\{\mathbf{k}_1,\ldots,\mathbf{k}_m\}$, we use the parameter-free fusion operator $\mathbf{F}_{attn}$. It assigns temperature-scaled similarity weights,
\begin{equation}
\alpha_i=\frac{\exp(\mathbf{q}^{\top}\mathbf{k}_i/\tau)}{\sum_{j=1}^{m}\exp(\mathbf{q}^{\top}\mathbf{k}_j/\tau)},
\qquad
\mathbf{F}_{attn}(\mathbf{q},K)=\operatorname{norm}\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_i\mathbf{k}_i\right),
\end{equation}
where $\tau$ controls the attention sharpness and $\operatorname{norm}$ denotes $L_2$ normalization. The operator retains the similarity-based form of dot-product attention \citep{Vaswani2017Attention}, but omits trainable projections. It uses the target unit's raw embedding as a reference vector for weighting local context, thereby selecting relevant information while preserving the unit's original semantic anchor.

\subsubsection{Bottom-up factual support and top-down context}

\begin{wrapfigure}{L}{0.50\textwidth}
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.98\linewidth]{figs/asymmetric_bidirectional_cascaded_index.pdf}
\caption{Asymmetric bidirectional index construction. Final parent representations flow downward as context, while only raw child representations flow upward as factual support. Each fusion operation remains anchored by the target's raw representation.}
\label{fig:asymmetric_index}
\end{wrapfigure}

Figure~\ref{fig:asymmetric_index} shows the index construction. The cascade proceeds from chunks to sentences to entities. At each level, raw child representations provide bottom-up factual support, whereas final parent representations provide top-down contextual guidance. This asymmetry prevents contextual information from being repeatedly fed back as factual evidence in subsequent cascade updates.

According to Appendix~\ref{app:latent_topic_construction}, let $\mathcal{T}(c_i)=\{k\mid c_i\in e_k^{T\rightarrow C}\}$ denote the set of topic indices associated with chunk $c_i$. For each $k\in\mathcal{T}(c_i)$, $m_{ik}$ denotes the fuzzy membership of $c_i$ in topic $k$, and $\mathbf{t}_k$ denotes the corresponding topic-anchor representation. We first form the membership-weighted topic context
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{topic}}=
\operatorname{norm}\!\left(
\frac{\sum_{k\in\mathcal{T}(c_i)}m_{ik}\mathbf{t}_k}
{\sum_{k\in\mathcal{T}(c_i)}m_{ik}}
\right).
\end{equation}
Let $\mathbf{h}_{s\_agg}^{(raw)}$ be the mean raw representation of sentences in $c$. The final chunk representation is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}_{c}^{(final)}=
\mathbf{F}_{attn}\!\bigl(\mathbf{h}_{c}^{(raw)},
\{\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{topic}},\mathbf{h}_{s\_agg}^{(raw)},\mathbf{h}_{c}^{(raw)}\}\bigr).
\end{equation}

A sentence can occur in overlapping chunks. We therefore aggregate the final representations of all parent chunks $\mathcal{P}(s)$,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}_{c\_agg}^{(final)}(s)=
\frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}(s)|}\sum_{c\in\mathcal{P}(s)}\mathbf{h}_{c}^{(final)}.
\end{equation}
Let $\mathbf{h}_{e\_agg}^{(raw)}(s)$ be the importance-weighted mean of the raw entities in $s$. The sentence update combines parent context, entity support, and its raw anchor:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}_{s}^{(final)}=
\mathbf{F}_{attn}\!\left(
\mathbf{h}_{s}^{(raw)},
\{\mathbf{h}_{c\_agg}^{(final)}(s),\mathbf{h}_{e\_agg}^{(raw)}(s),\mathbf{h}_{s}^{(raw)}\}
\right).
\end{equation}
Finally, an entity has no lower-level children. It receives only its final parent-sentence context while retaining its raw representation:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}_{e}^{(final)}=
\mathbf{F}_{attn}\!\left(
\mathbf{h}_{e}^{(raw)},
\{\mathbf{h}_{s}^{(final)},\mathbf{h}_{e}^{(raw)}\}
\right).
\end{equation}
Because the parent sentence already incorporates chunk- and topic-level context, this update can assign distinct representations to the same surface entity in different document contexts. Appendix~\ref{app:same_entity_different_sense_disambiguation} provides a contextual entity-disambiguation diagnostic for this capability.
Overall, the completed index couples broad context with local factual signals without requiring supervised graph training.

\subsection{Query-side Cascaded Enhancement and Retrieval}

\begin{wrapfigure}{R}{0.50\textwidth}
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.98\linewidth]{figs/Multi-hop retrieval and generation.pdf}
\caption{Multi-granularity retrieval and generation. Topic-guided chunk recall restricts the search space, three-level sentence reranking selects direct evidence, and shared-entity expansion retrieves multi-hop support for generation.}
\label{fig:multihop_retrieval_generation}
\end{wrapfigure}

The document index is structurally rich, whereas an input query is often short and underspecified. We therefore expand the query into \(\mathbf{q}^{(exp)}\), extract its entity set \(\mathcal{E}_q\), and construct query representations that correspond to the document levels. Query expansion follows the motivation of hypothetical-document methods, which reduce the semantic mismatch between query and document representations \citep{Gao2023HyDE}. Figure~\ref{fig:multihop_retrieval_generation} provides an overview of the resulting retrieval and generation process.

The expanded query first activates relevant topic anchors. Its score for topic $k$ is $r_{qk}={\mathbf{q}^{(exp)}}^{\top}\mathbf{t}_k$. Given $r_q^{\max}=\max_k r_{qk}$, the active set is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{A}_q=\{k\mid r_{qk}\geq\lambda r_q^{\max}\}.
\end{equation}
Their score-weighted mean defines the topic context $\mathbf{q}_{topic}$, and its associated topic--chunk hyperedges restrict the candidate chunks to a local subgraph. The raw expanded query remains the shared anchor for the chunk- and sentence-level representations:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{q}_{c}=\mathbf{F}_{attn}\!\left(
\mathbf{q}^{(exp)},\{\mathbf{q}_{topic},\mathbf{q}^{(exp)}\}\right),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{q}_{s}=\mathbf{F}_{attn}\!\left(
\mathbf{q}^{(exp)},\{\mathbf{q}_{c},\mathbf{q}_{e\_agg},\mathbf{q}^{(exp)}\}\right),
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{q}_{e\_agg}$ is the importance-weighted mean of raw query-entity representations. For each query entity $e_q\in\mathcal{E}_q$, we retain its raw representation as the fusion anchor and condition it on the final sentence-level query representation:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}_{e_q}^{(final)}=\mathbf{F}_{attn}\!\left(
\mathbf{h}_{e_q}^{(raw)},\{\mathbf{q}_{s},\mathbf{h}_{e_q}^{(raw)}\}\right).
\end{equation}
The final query-entity set is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{E}_q^{(final)}=\{\mathbf{h}_{e_q}^{(final)}\mid e_q\in\mathcal{E}_q\}.
\end{equation}

We first rank candidate chunks by
\begin{equation}
r_c={\mathbf{q}_{c}}^{\top}\mathbf{h}_{c}^{(final)},
\end{equation}
and retain the top-$K$ chunks. Within this scope, we rerank candidate sentences. Each sentence receives a contextual chunk score $s_c={\mathbf{q}^{(exp)}}^{\top}\mathbf{h}_{c}^{(final)}$ and a direct sentence score $s_s={\mathbf{q}^{(exp)}}^{\top}\mathbf{h}_{s}^{(final)}$. We use $\mathbf{q}^{(exp)}$ for these scores because it remains the stable lexical-semantic anchor before contextual query fusion. Appendix~\ref{app:query_scoring_comparison} compares this choice with cascaded query representations.

Let $\mathcal{E}_s$ denote the entity set extracted from a candidate sentence and $\mathbf{E}_s^{(final)}$ its final representations. The entity score retains fine-grained alignments through MaxSim \citep{Santhanam2022ColBERTv2}:
\begin{equation}
s_e=\frac{1}{|\mathbf{E}_q^{(final)}|}
\sum_{e_q\in\mathcal{E}_q}
\max_{e_d\in\mathcal{E}_s}
{\mathbf{h}_{e_q}^{(final)}}^{\top}\mathbf{h}_{e_d}^{(final)}.
\end{equation}
We obtain adaptive granularity weights from $[s_c,s_s,s_e]$ using a temperature-scaled Softmax. The reranking score is

To retrieve distributed support, we alternate between selected sentences and their entities. Each entity is used to look up unvisited sentences sharing that entity; their entities then seed the next expansion step. This procedure recovers evidence chains across physical chunks, as required in multi-hop question answering \citep{Yang2018HotpotQA}. The final generator receives two complementary tracks: top-ranked chunks establish topical scope and background, while reranked and multi-hop sentences provide fine-grained support. Multi-hop candidates are retained only when they concern the query entities and remain consistent with the direct sentence and chunk evidence. The answer is consequently grounded in mutually supported contextual and factual evidence.

\section{Experiments}\label{sec:experiments}
\subsection{Experimental Setup}

We evaluate eight document QA datasets spanning five machine-tool and three general domains. Among these datasets, the Mechanical dataset contains three 50-question stages requiring direct evidence, one intermediate connection, and two connected evidence links. Baselines include an LLM without retrieval, dense RAG \citep{Karpukhin2020DPR}, GraphRAG \citep{Edge2025GraphRAG}, LightRAG \citep{Guo2025LightRAG}, HippoRAG~2 \citep{Gutierrez2025HippoRAG2}, and Hyper-RAG \citep{Feng2026HyperRAG}. Within each comparison, all methods share the documents, questions, reference evidence, generator, and evaluation protocol. GPT-4o-mini generates questions and judges answers against reference evidence on six criteria scored from 0 to 100. We report mean $\pm$ standard deviation over five independent judging runs; the average score is the mean across the six criteria. Appendix~\ref{app:experimental_setup} provides dataset statistics, preprocessing, and the detailed evaluation protocol; Appendix~\ref{app:retrieval_efficiency} reports retrieval efficiency.
\subsection{Overall Performance Across RAG Methods}

Figure~\ref{fig:mechanical_llm_overall} reports Stage~1 results under GLM-4.7, Kimi-K2.5, Qwen3.5-Flash, GPT-4o-mini, and DeepSeek-V3.2. CascHyper-RAG ranks first under every backbone, with average scores of 77.2, 85.7, 88.3, 86.5, and 86.7. Its margin over the strongest baseline is 1.1--2.9 points across the five backbones. Absolute scores vary with the generator, from 77.2 under GLM-4.7 to 88.3 under Qwen3.5-Flash, but the ordering remains unchanged. Thus, the retrieval gain persists across generators with different answer capabilities.

Mechanical also provides a controlled comparison across evidence depths. Appendix Table~\ref{tab:mechanical_question_examples} illustrates direct factual support (Stage~1), one intermediate connection (Stage~2), and two connected evidence links (Stage~3). This progression tests whether a retriever can retain accurate support as questions require increasingly distributed evidence.

We next fix the generator and vary evidence depth. In Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanical_stage_overall}, CascHyper-RAG obtains 86.5, 88.9, and 88.1 on Stages~1--3, respectively. Hyper-RAG is the strongest competing baseline at each stage, reaching 83.7, 86.3, and 85.2. The corresponding gains are 2.8, 2.6, and 2.9 points. CascHyper-RAG therefore improves answer quality for both direct and multi-hop questions rather than only for one evidence depth.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

CascHyper-RAG organizes document evidence as nested topic--chunk, chunk--sentence, and sentence--entity hypergraphs, constructs an asymmetric bidirectional index, and applies cascaded multi-granularity enhancement to queries. Across eight document QA datasets, five LLM backbones, and three Mechanical question stages, it achieved the highest average performance and raised full-chain evidence recall from 53.8\% to 74.8\%. These results show that this hierarchical construction and multi-granularity evidence organization improve retrieval performance.

\section{Latent Topic Construction from Chunk Similarity}
\label{app:latent_topic_construction}

This appendix specifies how latent topics and their topic--chunk hyperedges are constructed. Let $\mathcal{C}=\{c_i\}_{i=1}^{N}$ denote the set of chunks, and let $\mathbf{x}_i\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ be the $L_2$-normalized raw embedding of chunk $c_i$. The pairwise semantic similarity between chunks is therefore computed by the dot product
\begin{equation}
s_{ij}=\mathbf{x}_i^{\top}\mathbf{x}_j,
\quad i\neq j.
\end{equation}
To retain only informative semantic connections, we compute a dynamic threshold from the upper-triangular similarity values:
\begin{equation}
\tau=\operatorname{mean}\left(\{s_{ij}\mid i<j\}\right)
+0.5\operatorname{std}\left(\{s_{ij}\mid i<j\}\right).
\end{equation}
We then construct a weighted undirected chunk-similarity graph $G=(\mathcal{C},E)$, where
\begin{equation}
(c_i,c_j)\in E\ \Longleftrightarrow\ s_{ij}>\tau,
\qquad w_{ij}=s_{ij}.
\end{equation}
Leiden community detection is applied to $G$ with edge weights $w_{ij}$ and resolution parameter $1.0$, producing a partition $\{C_k\}_{k=1}^{K}$ of the chunks \citep{Traag2019Leiden}. Each community defines one latent topic. Its topic-anchor representation is the normalized centroid of the member chunk embeddings:
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\mu}_k=\frac{1}{|C_k|}\sum_{c_i\in C_k}\mathbf{x}_i,
\qquad
\mathbf{t}_k=\frac{\boldsymbol{\mu}_k}{\|\boldsymbol{\mu}_k\|_2}.
\end{equation}

Although the Leiden partition provides a primary topic assignment, topic membership is made fuzzy to preserve cross-topic associations. For each chunk $c_i$ and topic anchor $\mathbf{t}_k$, we compute
\begin{equation}
m_{ik}=\mathbf{x}_i^{\top}\mathbf{t}_k.
\end{equation}
If $k$ is the primary Leiden community of $c_i$, we set $m_{ik}=1$. For every other topic with $m_{ik}>\eta$, where $\eta$ is a fuzzy-membership threshold, the chunk is assigned an additional membership with weight $m_{ik}$. The topic--chunk hyperedge associated with topic $k$ is thus
\begin{equation}
e_k^{T\rightarrow C}=\left\{c_i\in\mathcal{C}\mid m_{ik}>\eta\ \text{or}\ k=\operatorname{Leiden}(c_i)\right\}.
\end{equation}
Consequently, each hyperedge represents a latent topic as a set of semantically related chunks, while a chunk may participate in multiple topic hyperedges when it has sufficiently high similarity to multiple topic anchors.

\section{Diagnostic for Same-Entity Different-Sense Disambiguation Across Document Contexts}
\label{app:same_entity_different_sense_disambiguation}

To provide a targeted diagnostic for same-entity different-sense disambiguation across document contexts, we constructed a balanced set of 150 entity pairs from the fixed multi-domain corpus, comprising 75 same-sense pairs and 75 different-sense pairs. Each pair contains two occurrences of the same entity in different document contexts. Each occurrence was used only as a query-time context over the fixed corpus index. We treated different-sense pairs as positive instances and used cosine distance between the two contextualized representations as the disambiguation score. We report AUROC with 95\% confidence intervals obtained from 2,000 stratified bootstrap samples.

We compare a Raw condition, which uses only the static embedding of the target entity, with the Full cascade condition, which contextualizes the target using topic routing and retrieved chunk-, sentence-, and entity-level evidence. As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:same_entity_different_sense_disambiguation}, Raw matching cannot distinguish the two classes because identical entities receive identical static embeddings. In contrast, Full cascade achieves an AUROC of 0.774, with a 95\% confidence interval of $[0.691, 0.854]$. Its paired AUROC improvement over Raw is 0.274, with a 95\% bootstrap interval of $[0.191, 0.354]$. These results indicate that query-time cascaded contextualization can distinguish entities used with different meanings in the evaluated corpus.

Each point in Fig.~\ref{fig:retrieval_efficiency} combines the mean latency of a retrieval method with its average answer-quality score.

\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.62\linewidth]{figs/retrieval_efficiency_mechanical.pdf}
\caption{Average retrieval efficiency on 50 first-stage mechanical questions. Each point reports the average retrieval time and the corresponding average answer-quality score of a retrieval method.}
\label{fig:retrieval_efficiency}
\end{figure}

RAG is fastest because it directly matches text chunks. LightRAG is also relatively efficient, whereas Hyper-RAG and CascHyper-RAG spend more time on structured retrieval. CascHyper-RAG achieves the highest answer-quality score while retaining latency comparable to Hyper-RAG. Its additional topic-guided recall, sentence reranking, and multi-hop expansion therefore improve evidence organization without a large latency increase over the strongest structured-retrieval baseline.
