\begin{abstract}
Retrieval-augmented generation systems often retrieve evidence from flat chunk spaces, overlooking the organizational relationships among semantic units at different document levels. This paper proposes HoloHyper RAG, a holistic cascaded multi-granularity hypergraph framework for retrieval-augmented knowledge question answering. The framework models topics, chunks, sentences, and sentence-extracted entities as semantically organized levels, linked through nested topic--chunk, chunk--sentence, and sentence--entity relations. It builds context-aware representations through an asymmetric bidirectional cascaded index with local attention fusion. At retrieval time, the expanded query first undergoes cascaded enhancement, followed by multi-granularity retrieval and generation. Candidate sentences are reranked by dynamically fusing chunk-level semantic matching, sentence-level semantic matching, and entity-level MaxSim matching scores. Associated evidence is retrieved through multi-hop expansion over shared entities and used for dual-track contrastive generation. Experiments are conducted on eight document question-answering datasets, including five machine-tool subsets and three general-domain subsets. HoloHyper RAG is compared with LLM, dense retrieval, graph-enhanced RAG, and hypergraph-based RAG baselines under matched corpora, questions, embedding models, and evaluation protocols. The results show that the proposed framework improves evidence organization for direct factual questions and questions requiring connected evidence links, remains competitive across domains and LLM backbones, and achieves the highest average score in the ablation setting while keeping retrieval time comparable to the HyperRAG baseline. These findings indicate that explicitly modeling and preserving cross-level document relationships provides an effective basis for integrating global contextual guidance with fine-grained factual evidence in knowledge-intensive RAG.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Large language models (LLMs) have become a general interface for knowledge-intensive question answering, but their parametric memory is incomplete, difficult to update, and prone to unsupported generation. Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) addresses this limitation by retrieving external evidence before generation, allowing the model to condition its answer on non-parametric knowledge sources \citep{Guu2020REALM,Lewis2020RAG,Karpukhin2020DPR,Huang2026RAGSurvey}. This paradigm is particularly relevant to domain-specific question answering, where answers often depend on technical terms, document-specific constraints, and recently updated knowledge \citep{Yang2025KBSLLMKB}. However, improving answer quality in such settings requires retrieval representations that capture document semantic organization rather than relying on semantic similarity alone.

Conventional chunk-based RAG systems usually retrieve documents as collections of independent units through dense similarity matching. This design is efficient, but it overlooks the fact that documents comprise semantically organized units at multiple levels, including topics, chunks, sentences, and entities. Topics organize chunks, chunks contextualize sentences, and sentences associate entities within local factual contexts. Flat retrieval representations do not explicitly preserve these organizational relationships. Graph-augmented RAG methods relax this independence assumption by introducing entities, relations, communities, or knowledge graphs into retrieval pipelines \citep{Pan2024LLMKG,Jin2024LLMGraphs,Edge2025GraphRAG,Xu2024KGRAGCSQA,Guo2025LightRAG}. Nevertheless, pairwise graph structures and coarse graph summaries may not adequately preserve the organizational relationships among topics, chunks, sentences, and entities that are needed for multi-granularity evidence retrieval.

The central challenge is therefore to construct a retrieval representation that explicitly preserves and exploits these cross-level organizational relationships. Such a representation should combine higher-level semantic context with fine-grained factual evidence to improve evidence localization and integration. Hypergraphs provide a natural way to model such high-order relations because one hyperedge can connect more than two semantic units \citep{Feng2019HGNN,Gao2023HGNNPlus}. This property has motivated broader work on graph and hypergraph representation learning for non-Euclidean and high-order data \citep{Wu2021GNNSurvey}. Recent hypergraph-based RAG studies further suggest that hypergraph structures can represent multi-entity evidence more flexibly than ordinary graphs \citep{Feng2026HyperRAG}. However, existing hypergraph-based RAG does not typically preserve this multi-level structure throughout both indexing and query-time retrieval. This requirement remains insufficiently explored.

To address this gap, this paper proposes HoloHyper RAG, a cascaded multi-granularity hypergraph framework for knowledge question answering. The framework explicitly models topics, chunks, sentences, and sentence-extracted entities as semantically organized levels, connected through nested topic--chunk, chunk--sentence, and sentence--entity sub-hypergraphs. It builds context-aware representations through an asymmetric bidirectional cascaded index with local attention fusion, preserving local factual signals while incorporating higher-level context. At retrieval time, the expanded query undergoes cascaded enhancement to align it with the document representation. Retrieval then progressively identifies topic anchors, candidate chunks within the corresponding scope, and candidate sentences containing directly relevant evidence. Candidate sentences are reranked by dynamically fusing chunk-level semantic matching, sentence-level semantic matching, and entity-level MaxSim matching scores. Associated evidence is retrieved through multi-hop expansion over shared entities and used for dual-track contrastive generation. By preserving and exploiting cross-level document relationships throughout the pipeline, HoloHyper RAG supports the identification and integration of evidence distributed across textual units.

The main contributions of this work are summarized as follows. First, we construct a cascaded multi-granularity hypergraph over topics, chunks, sentences, and sentence-extracted entities. It comprises nested topic--chunk, chunk--sentence, and sentence--entity sub-hypergraphs. Second, we construct an asymmetric bidirectional cascaded index based on local attention fusion. Bottom-up aggregation uses the original representations of associated lower-level units to preserve local facts. Top-down contextualization passes each final higher-level representation to its associated lower-level units, allowing topical, chunk, and sentence context to enrich lower-level representations without overwriting their raw factual signals. Third, we introduce a query-side cascaded enhancement and multi-granularity retrieval and generation procedure. It first performs cascaded enhancement to construct aligned multi-granularity query representation. During retrieval, the enhanced query first identifies relevant topic anchors, retrieves candidate chunks within the corresponding topic scope, and then recalls candidate sentences from those chunks. Candidate sentences are then reranked by dynamically fusing chunk-level semantic matching, sentence-level semantic matching, and entity-level MaxSim matching scores. Associated evidence is retrieved through multi-hop expansion over shared entities and used for dual-track contrastive generation. Finally, we evaluate the full framework on eight document question-answering datasets, including five machine-tool and three general-domain subsets. The experiments include baseline comparison, cross-domain evaluation, component ablation, and retrieval efficiency analysis under matched corpora, questions, embedding models, and evaluation protocols.

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Question Answering}

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) integrates external knowledge retrieval with language generation and has become a common approach to knowledge-intensive question answering. Early retrieval-augmented language models retrieved latent documents or passages during pre-training and generation, showing that non-parametric memory can improve factual grounding \citep{Guu2020REALM,Lewis2020RAG}. Dense passage retrieval further improved open-domain question answering by replacing sparse lexical matching with learned semantic retrieval \citep{Karpukhin2020DPR}. Recent surveys organize RAG systems around pre-retrieval, retrieval, post-retrieval, and generation stages, and emphasize their role in reducing outdated or unsupported generation \citep{Gao2024RAGSurvey,Huang2026RAGSurvey}. Multi-level retrieval has also been explored by combining evidence from different information sources for knowledge-based visual question answering \citep{Adjali2024MultiLevelRAG}. Complex knowledge base question answering studies further show that multi-hop questions require reasoning over connected evidence rather than isolated facts \citep{Lan2023ComplexKBQA}. These works provide the retrieval and generation foundation for this paper. However, they do not explicitly model how topics, chunks, sentences, and entities are organizationally related within a document, nor do they preserve these cross-level relationships in a cascaded index for query-time evidence selection.

\subsection{Graph-based Retrieval-Augmented Generation}

Graph-based retrieval methods introduce explicit structure into RAG pipelines by organizing entities, relations, communities, or knowledge graphs around the retrieved evidence. Knowledge graphs have been studied as a complementary representation for LLMs because they store factual relations explicitly and can improve interpretability and knowledge access \citep{Pan2024LLMKG,Yang2025KBSLLMKB}. Broader surveys on LLMs over graphs and automatic knowledge graph construction further show that graph structures are useful when text contains relational or entity-centered dependencies \citep{Jin2024LLMGraphs,Zhong2023KGConstruction}. In RAG, GraphRAG builds entity graphs and community summaries for query-focused summarization, while LightRAG and knowledge-graph RAG variants use graph structures to improve retrieval efficiency and factual association \citep{Edge2025GraphRAG,Guo2025LightRAG,Xu2024KGRAGCSQA,Peng2025GraphRAGSurvey}. However, graph-enhanced systems usually focus on pairwise entity relations, extracted relations, or community-level summaries. They do not explicitly represent the organizational chain through which topics delimit chunks, chunks contextualize sentences, and sentences ground entities. The proposed framework retains structural retrieval but extends it from pairwise graph organization to a cascaded multi-granularity hypergraph that preserves these cross-level relationships.

\subsection{Hypergraph Retrieval-Augmented Generation}

Hypergraphs provide a natural representation for high-order relations because a hyperedge can connect multiple vertices within one semantic event or context. Hypergraph neural networks and their general extensions have shown that hypergraph learning can capture higher-order dependencies beyond ordinary pairwise graphs \citep{Feng2019HGNN,Gao2023HGNNPlus}. Graph neural network surveys also highlight the importance of representation learning on non-Euclidean structures when data contain complex relational patterns \citep{Wu2021GNNSurvey}. Hypergraph-based RAG work applies this idea to retrieval by representing multi-entity knowledge with hypergraph structures \citep{Feng2026HyperRAG}. These studies motivate the use of hypergraphs for evidence organization, but they generally focus on hyperedges over entities or other single-level semantic units. They do not explicitly model the nested organizational relationships from topics to chunks, sentences, and entities, or preserve these relationships throughout indexing and retrieval. HoloHyper RAG differs by constructing this cascade as part of the document index and using asymmetric local attention fusion to propagate higher-level context while retaining lower-level factual anchors.

\section{Method}

Figure~\ref{fig:holohyper_overall_framework} presents an overview of HoloHyper RAG, which consists of a document-indexing stage and a query-side retrieval-and-generation stage. During document indexing, the corpus is segmented into topics, chunks, sentences, and sentence-level entities, which are organized into a cascaded multi-granularity hypergraph. By constructing an asymmetric bidirectional cascaded index with local attention fusion, the framework obtains context-aware representations across these semantic levels. At query time, the user query first undergoes cascaded query enhancement, followed by multi-granularity retrieval and generation. Relevant chunks are recalled within the topic scope. Candidate sentences are reranked by jointly considering chunk-, sentence-, and entity-level signals, and shared entities support multi-hop evidence retrieval. The retrieved evidence is finally used for dual-track contrastive generation. The subsequent sections provide a detailed description of each component and process in HoloHyper RAG.

\subsection{Cascaded Multi-granularity Hypergraph Construction}
Existing methods, such as LightRAG and GraphRAG, usually organize entities and text chunks within a flat structure, which may lead to a separation between macro-level contextual semantics and micro-level factual details \citep{Edge2025GraphRAG,Guo2025LightRAG,Feng2026HyperRAG}. To reconstruct the intrinsic hierarchical structure of textual knowledge, we propose a top-down cascaded multi-granularity hypergraph framework. The framework consists of four semantic levels and three nested hypergraph relationships, covering topics ($\mathbf{T}$), chunks ($\mathbf{C}$), sentences ($\mathbf{S}$), and sentence-level entities ($\mathbf{E}$).

In a standard hypergraph, a hyperedge is defined as a subset of vertices, which allows one relation to involve more than two semantic units \citep{Zhou2006Hypergraphs,Feng2019HGNN,Gao2023HGNNPlus,Chien2022AllSet}. In the proposed framework, semantic units at different granularities dynamically play the roles of either vertices or hyperedges. Specifically, as illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:hypergraph_construction}, the overall structure is composed of three cascaded sub-hypergraphs.


\begin{figure}[pos=t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figs/cascaded_multigranularity_hypergraph_construction.pdf}
\caption{Cascaded multi-granularity hypergraph construction. Topics act as hyperedges over chunk vertices, chunks act as hyperedges over sentence vertices, and sentences act as hyperedges over entity vertices. Overlapping memberships preserve fuzzy topic associations and local textual boundaries.}
\label{fig:hypergraph_construction}
\end{figure}

First, in the topic--chunk hypergraph, chunks are treated as vertices, while latent topics are modeled as hyperedges. A topic is a latent semantic unit inferred from the distribution of chunk representations rather than a predefined document label. The topics are discovered by applying the Leiden algorithm to cluster chunk representations in a high-dimensional semantic space \citep{Blondel2008Louvain,Traag2019Leiden}. Each discovered topic cluster is defined as a high-order hyperedge. According to semantic similarity, a topic hyperedge connects a set of chunks that share similar contextual semantics. The construction of the semantic-similarity graph, Leiden partition, and fuzzy topic memberships is detailed in Appendix~\ref{app:latent_topic_construction}. This design allows one chunk vertex to belong to multiple topic hyperedges, thereby capturing fuzzy topic associations across physically distant text regions.

Second, in the chunk--sentence hypergraph, natural sentences are treated as vertices, while physical chunks are modeled as hyperedges. Here and below, a sentence denotes a sentence-level semantic unit, which may consist of one or more consecutive natural sentences when they jointly express a complete idea; the corresponding extraction prompt is provided in Appendix~\ref{app:sentence_extraction_prompt}. In this sub-hypergraph, each chunk hyperedge connects all sentences located within the same physical text block. This preserves the local textual order and explicit physical boundaries of the original document.

Third, in the sentence--entity hypergraph, fine-grained atomic knowledge units are treated as vertices, including extracted entity names, actions, intentions, states, and other key semantic elements, while natural sentences are modeled as hyperedges. The entity-extraction prompt is provided in Appendix~\ref{app:entity_extraction_prompt}. At the micro level, each sentence hyperedge connects all entities extracted from the sentence. Each extracted entity is assigned an importance weight that reflects its role in the sentence and is used in the subsequent entity-level aggregation. In this way, originally isolated entity vertices are endowed with sentence-level co-occurrence patterns and logical constraints.

\subsection{Asymmetric Bidirectional Cascaded Index Construction Based on Local Attention Fusion}

\subsubsection{Local Attention Fusion Operator}

After constructing the cascaded hypergraph, it is necessary to generate node representations that are aware of macro-level contextual semantics. Traditional graph and hypergraph neural networks often rely on neighborhood propagation, Laplacian-based smoothing, or learned attention over adjacent nodes \citep{Kipf2017GCN,Hamilton2017GraphSAGE,Velickovic2018GAT,Feng2019HGNN}. These operations can be effective for supervised representation learning, but may cause feature over-smoothing when directly applied to sparse textual structures in a zero-shot retrieval setting. To better adapt to the sparse topology of the proposed hypergraph, we introduce a local attention fusion operator, denoted as $\mathbf{F}_{attn}$.

Let the raw representation of a target node be $\mathbf{q} \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$, and let its contextual feature set be $K=\{\mathbf{k}_1,\mathbf{k}_2,\ldots,\mathbf{k}_m\}$. The attention weight $\alpha_i$ is computed using temperature-scaled dot-product similarity:
\begin{equation}
\alpha_i =
\frac{
\exp\left(\mathbf{q}^{T}\mathbf{k}_i/\tau\right)
}{
\sum_{j=1}^{m} \exp\left(\mathbf{q}^{T}\mathbf{k}_j/\tau\right)
}.
\end{equation}
The fused feature is then normalized:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{F}_{attn}(\mathbf{q},K)
=
\frac{
\sum_{i=1}^{m} \alpha_i \mathbf{k}_i
}{
\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{m} \alpha_i \mathbf{k}_i\right\|_2
}.
\end{equation}
Here, $\mathbf{q}$ denotes the raw query vector of the target node, $\tau$ is a temperature coefficient controlling the smoothness of the attention distribution, and $\|\cdot\|_2$ denotes the $L_2$ norm.

The operator $\mathbf{F}_{attn}$ is inspired by the scaled dot-product attention mechanism in Transformers \citep{Vaswani2017Attention}. However, it performs parameter-free semantic fusion over local topological neighborhoods in a zero-shot retrieval setting rather than trainable sequence modeling. Unlike standard attention modules, $\mathbf{F}_{attn}$ does not introduce learnable Query, Key, or Value projection matrices. Instead, it computes the inner product between normalized embedding vectors, so that the attention score directly reflects their semantic similarity. At each cascaded level, the query vector is fixed as the node's original static representation $\mathbf{q}^{(raw)}$, keeping the attention weights conditioned on the target node's intrinsic semantic identity while selecting relevant contextual features. The operator can therefore be regarded as a local cross-attention mechanism: feature interactions are restricted by the incidence relationships of the nested hypergraph rather than propagated over the entire graph. A target node interacts only with its associated hyperedge environment or directly connected child nodes, resulting in a sparse topology-aware fusion mechanism that respects textual boundaries.

\subsubsection{Asymmetric Bidirectional Cascaded Index Construction}

Figure~\ref{fig:asymmetric_index} provides an overview of the asymmetric bidirectional cascaded index construction. Each local attention operation is anchored by the raw representation of its target node, namely the semantic unit currently being updated. Its associated child nodes are directly linked lower-level semantic units, whereas its parent node is the directly linked higher-level semantic unit. For example, a chunk receives topic-level context and aggregates sentence-level support, while a sentence receives chunk-level context and aggregates entity-level support. Along the bottom-up path, the model aggregates the raw static representations of associated child nodes, thereby allowing sentence- and entity-level factual information to provide local support for their respective parent representations. Along the top-down path, the final representation of each parent node after local attention fusion is provided to its associated lower-level child nodes as contextual input. Together, these opposing directional flows define the asymmetric bidirectional cascaded index described below.

\begin{figure}[pos=t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figs/asymmetric_bidirectional_cascaded_index.pdf}
\caption{Asymmetric bidirectional cascaded index construction. Top-down paths propagate final parent representations as macro-level context, whereas bottom-up paths use only raw child representations as factual support. Each $\mathbf{F}_{attn}$ operation uses the target node's raw representation as its semantic anchor.}
\label{fig:asymmetric_index}
\end{figure}

Latent topic anchors are first constructed from chunk similarity as described in Appendix~\ref{app:latent_topic_construction}. Let $\mathbf{T}$, $\mathbf{C}$, $\mathbf{S}$, and $\mathbf{E}$ denote the sets of latent topic anchors, chunks, sentences, and entities, respectively. Conditioned on the topic anchors in $\mathbf{T}$, feature updates proceed in the topological cascade order $\mathbf{C} \rightarrow \mathbf{S} \rightarrow \mathbf{E}$: topic representations provide context for chunk updates, and the resulting final chunk and sentence representations are successively passed to lower-level units.

At the chunk level, for each chunk $c_i \in \mathbf{C}$, the update process incorporates both latent topic guidance and fine-grained support from its contained sentences. Let $\mathcal{T}(c_i)=\{k\mid c_i\in e_k^{T\rightarrow C}\}$ denote the set of topic anchors associated with $c_i$. Using the topic-anchor representations $\mathbf{t}_k$ and fuzzy membership weights $m_{ik}$ defined in Appendix~\ref{app:latent_topic_construction}, the membership-weighted topic context is computed as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\bar{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathrm{topic}}
&=
\frac{\sum_{k\in\mathcal{T}(c_i)}m_{ik}\mathbf{t}_k}
{\sum_{k\in\mathcal{T}(c_i)}m_{ik}},\\
\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{topic}}
&=
\frac{\bar{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathrm{topic}}}
{\|\bar{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathrm{topic}}\|_2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The fusion context comprises the topic context vector $\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{topic}}$, the raw sentence aggregation vector $\mathbf{h}_{s\_agg}^{(raw)}$, obtained by averaging the raw representations of all sentences within the chunk, and the raw representation of the chunk itself $\mathbf{h}_{c}^{(raw)}$. The final chunk representation is computed as:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}_{c}^{(final)}
=
\mathbf{F}_{attn}
\left(
\mathbf{h}_{c}^{(raw)},
\left\{
\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{topic}},
\mathbf{h}_{s\_agg}^{(raw)},
\mathbf{h}_{c}^{(raw)}
\right\}
\right).
\end{equation}
Here, $\mathbf{F}_{attn}$ denotes the local attention fusion operator defined in Eqs.~(1) and (2).
The representation $\mathbf{h}_{c}^{(final)}$ denotes the final context-aware representation of chunk $c$ after fusing topic-level semantics, local sentence support, and its own raw semantics.

At the sentence level, for each sentence $s \in \mathbf{S}$, the received upper-level context is not the raw representation of its parent chunk, but the final chunk representation produced in the previous stage. Since overlapping windows are used during chunking, adjacent chunks may share textual content, and after sentence extraction and deduplication, the same sentence may be contained in multiple chunks, forming a multi-parent relationship. For such cases, we do not force a unique parent chunk. Instead, we aggregate the final representations of all parent chunks in $\mathcal{P}(s)$:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}_{c\_agg}^{(final)}
=
\frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}(s)|}
\sum_{c \in \mathcal{P}(s)}
\mathbf{h}_{c}^{(final)}.
\end{equation}
Here, $\mathcal{P}(s)$ denotes the set of chunks containing sentence $s$, and $\mathbf{h}_{c\_agg}^{(final)}$ denotes the aggregated parent-level contextual representation.

Meanwhile, the sentence node receives bottom-up factual support from its entity set. This support is computed by weighted aggregation of raw entity representations according to their importance weights and is denoted as $\mathbf{h}_{e\_agg}^{(raw)}$. Therefore, the sentence-level candidate feature set contains three components: the aggregated final parent-chunk representation $\mathbf{h}_{c\_agg}^{(final)}$, the raw entity aggregation vector $\mathbf{h}_{e\_agg}^{(raw)}$, and the raw sentence representation $\mathbf{h}_{s}^{(raw)}$. The final sentence representation is computed as:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}_{s}^{(final)}
=
\mathbf{F}_{attn}
\left(
\mathbf{h}_{s}^{(raw)},
\left\{
\mathbf{h}_{c\_agg}^{(final)},
\mathbf{h}_{e\_agg}^{(raw)},
\mathbf{h}_{s}^{(raw)}
\right\}
\right).
\end{equation}
Here, $\mathbf{h}_{s}^{(final)}$ denotes the final sentence representation after fusing parent-level macro-context, entity-level factual support, and its own raw semantics.

At the entity level, entities $e \in \mathbf{E}$ are bottom-level nodes and have no child nodes for further aggregation. Therefore, the update process only injects contextual information from the parent sentence while preserving the raw semantic anchor of the entity itself. The entity-level attention candidate set consists of the final representation of the parent sentence $\mathbf{h}_{s}^{(final)}$ and the raw entity representation $\mathbf{h}_{e}^{(raw)}$:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}_{e}^{(final)}
=
\mathbf{F}_{attn}
\left(
\mathbf{h}_{e}^{(raw)},
\left\{
\mathbf{h}_{s}^{(final)},
\mathbf{h}_{e}^{(raw)}
\right\}
\right).
\end{equation}
Here, $\mathbf{h}_{e}^{(final)}$ denotes the context-aware entity representation after sentence-level contextual injection.

At the entity level, the proposed update provides structural support for distinguishing entities that carry different meanings across document contexts. Static entity embeddings alone cannot encode the local context needed to differentiate homonymous or polysemous entities, whereas entity linking and contextual representation studies have shown that mention context is important for resolving such ambiguity \citep{Hoffart2011AIDA,Yamada2020LUKE,Sevgili2022EntityLinkingSurvey}. In our framework, the raw entity representation $\mathbf{h}_{e}^{(raw)}$ serves as the semantic anchor of $\mathbf{F}_{attn}$, while the final representation of its parent sentence, $\mathbf{h}_{s}^{(final)}$, provides contextual input that has already incorporated sentence-, chunk-, and topic-level information through the cascaded index. Consequently, an entity can obtain distinct context-aware representations when it occurs in different sentence, chunk, or topic environments. This parameter-free contextualization requires neither supervised fine-tuning nor external knowledge-graph alignment, and may reduce semantic ambiguity and matching errors in fine-grained retrieval. The same-entity different-sense disambiguation diagnostic in Appendix~\ref{app:same_entity_different_sense_disambiguation} provides empirical support for this capability.

\subsection{Query-side Cascaded Enhancement and Multi-granularity Retrieval and Generation}

After the asymmetric cascaded index is constructed, the document corpus is transformed into a context-aware multi-granularity nested hypergraph. However, on the retrieval side, user queries are usually short and lack sufficient background information and intent constraints. This creates a structural asymmetry between the original query and the structurally processed document representations. Existing studies have shown that query rewriting and hypothetical-document expansion can improve retrieval by enriching underspecified queries and bringing them into closer alignment with document representations in the retriever's semantic space \citep{Ma2023QueryRewriting,Gao2023HyDE}. To reduce this semantic asymmetry, we perform cascaded enhancement on user queries and conduct multi-granularity retrieval and generation.

\subsubsection{Query-side Cascaded Enhancement}

To measure query and document representations in a comparable semantic space, we perform cascaded enhancement on the user query in a manner corresponding to the document side, thereby achieving structural alignment between the two representations. First, a large language model is used to expand the original short query into an enriched query representation $\mathbf{q}^{(exp)}$ containing more background information and intent cues. Then, a query entity set $\mathbf{E}_{q}$ is extracted from the expanded query using the same entity extraction procedure as on the document side, and each query entity is assigned an importance weight. For each query entity $e_q \in \mathbf{E}_{q}$, its raw representation $\mathbf{h}_{e_q}^{(raw)}$ is obtained, and the query entity aggregation vector $\mathbf{q}_{e\_agg}$ is computed by weighted aggregation of these entity representations.

Next, $\mathbf{q}^{(exp)}$ is projected into the representation space of the latent topic anchors in $\mathbf{T}$, and its matching score with each topic anchor is computed as
\begin{equation}
r_{qk}
=
\left(\mathbf{q}^{(exp)}\right)^{\top}\mathbf{t}_{k},
\quad k\in\mathbf{T}.
\end{equation}
Let $r_q^{\max}=\max_{k\in\mathbf{T}}r_{qk}$. The system adaptively activates topic anchors whose scores satisfy a relative threshold:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{A}_{q}
=
\left\{
k\in\mathbf{T}
\mid
r_{qk}\geq\lambda r_q^{\max}
\right\},
\end{equation}
Next, the activated topic anchors are fused into a macro-level topic guidance vector using score-weighted aggregation:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{q}_{topic}
=
\operatorname{norm}
\left(
\dfrac{\sum_{k\in\mathcal{A}_{q}}r_{qk}\mathbf{t}_{k}}
{\sum_{k\in\mathcal{A}_{q}}r_{qk}+\epsilon}
\right).
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{norm}(\cdot)$ denotes $L_2$ normalization and $\epsilon$ is a small constant introduced for numerical stability. The subsequent candidate search is restricted to the local chunk subgraph associated with the activated topics. This design transforms unconstrained global chunk retrieval into topic-guided local candidate recall, following the general observation that retrieval systems benefit when the candidate space is pruned before fine-grained reranking.

Because user queries are usually single sentences or short texts, we treat the expanded query as both a chunk-level and a sentence-level semantic unit. At the chunk level, it absorbs aggregated semantics from the matched fuzzy topic anchors. At the sentence level, it incorporates the resulting chunk-level representation and aggregated entity semantics. Thus, query-side cascaded enhancement does not re-segment the query into paragraphs or sentences. Instead, it maps the same expanded query representation into different granularity spaces with different semantic roles.

Based on this design, the query side performs cascaded enhancement corresponding to the document side. The fuzzy topics first provide macro-level guidance. At the chunk level, the expanded query is fused with the topic guidance vector through local attention to form the final chunk-level query representation:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{q}_{c}
=
\mathbf{F}_{attn}
\left(
\mathbf{q}^{(exp)},
\left\{
\mathbf{q}_{topic},
\mathbf{q}^{(exp)}
\right\}
\right).
\end{equation}

At the sentence level, the expanded query representation is further fused with the query entity aggregation representation and the final chunk-level query representation to form the final sentence-level query representation:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{q}_{s}
=
\mathbf{F}_{attn}
\left(
\mathbf{q}^{(exp)},
\left\{
\mathbf{q}_{c},
\mathbf{q}_{e\_agg},
\mathbf{q}^{(exp)}
\right\}
\right).
\end{equation}

Furthermore, each query entity uses its own raw semantics as an anchor and absorbs the final sentence-level query representation to form a final entity-level query representation:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}_{e_q}^{(final)}
=
\mathbf{F}_{attn}
\left(
\mathbf{h}_{e_q}^{(raw)},
\left\{
\mathbf{q}_{s},
\mathbf{h}_{e_q}^{(raw)}
\right\}
\right),
\quad e_q \in \mathbf{E}_{q}.
\end{equation}

Collecting the final representations of all query entities yields the final query entity representation set:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{E}_{q}^{(final)}
=
\left\{
\mathbf{h}_{e_q}^{(final)}
\mid
e_q \in \mathbf{E}_{q}
\right\}.
\end{equation}
Here, $\mathbf{q}_{c}$ denotes the final chunk-level query representation, $\mathbf{q}_{s}$ denotes the final sentence-level query representation, and $\mathbf{E}_{q}^{(final)}$ denotes the set of final entity-level query representations. Through this structure-aligned cascaded enhancement, the original flat query is transformed into a multi-granularity query representation.

\subsubsection{Multi-granularity Retrieval and Generation}

Figure~\ref{fig:multihop_retrieval_generation} illustrates the multi-granularity retrieval and generation process. The enhanced query first recalls candidate chunks within the topic-guided scope. Candidate sentences are then reranked by fusing chunk-level, sentence-level, and entity-level semantic matching scores. Multi-hop expansion over shared entities retrieves associated evidence, and the selected evidence is finally supplied to the dual-track contrastive generation to produce the answer. The following subsections describe the individual retrieval and generation stages in detail.

\begin{figure}[pos=t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figs/Multi-hop retrieval and generation.pdf}
\caption{Multi-granularity retrieval and generation. Topic-guided chunk recall, multi-granularity sentence reranking, and multi-hop retrieval  retrieve the evidence. The selected evidence is used for dual-track contrastive generation.}
\label{fig:multihop_retrieval_generation}
\end{figure}

The retrieval process first performs coarse-grained chunk recall within the topic-pruned local chunk set. The chunk-level recall score is computed by matching the final chunk-level query representation $\mathbf{q}_{c}$ with the final chunk representation:
\begin{equation}
r_{c}
=
\mathbf{q}_{c}^{T}
\mathbf{h}_{c}^{(final)}.
\end{equation}
The Top-K chunks are then selected as the candidate scope for subsequent sentence-level reranking. This coarse-grained stage restricts the search to a topic-relevant local region, thereby reducing the cost and noise of sentence-level comparison. However, a relevant chunk may contain multiple sentences, only some of which directly support the query; therefore, chunk-level recall alone is insufficient for selecting the final evidence.

Sentence-level retrieval is therefore performed within the candidate chunks to distinguish directly relevant evidence from other locally related content. Each candidate sentence is reranked using three complementary scores: a chunk-level contextual score, a sentence-level semantic score, and an entity-level MaxSim score. The chunk-level score retains macro-level topical context, the sentence-level score measures semantic correspondence with the candidate sentence itself, and the entity-level score preserves fine-grained factual matching between query and candidate-sentence entities. Dynamically fusing these scores adjusts their relative contributions to the information need: queries seeking an overall explanation can place greater emphasis on contextual and sentence-level signals, whereas queries seeking specific facts can place greater emphasis on entity-level matching. We use the expanded query vector $\mathbf{q}^{(exp)}$ as a stable semantic anchor for the chunk- and sentence-level matching scores, rather than directly using $\mathbf{q}_{s}$ for matching. The vector $\mathbf{q}_{s}$ is mainly used to provide sentence-level context for query entities and to generate final entity-level query representations. Since $\mathbf{q}_{s}$ has already fused the final chunk-level query representation $\mathbf{q}_{c}$ and the query entity aggregation vector $\mathbf{q}_{e\_agg}$, it contains a relatively strong contextual bias. Directly matching $\mathbf{q}_{s}$ with candidate sentences may overemphasize topic or entity priors and amplify semantic drift during multi-hop retrieval. Therefore, $\mathbf{q}^{(exp)}$ is retained as the stable query anchor for sentence reranking; Appendix~\ref{app:query_scoring_comparison} reports a controlled comparison experiment supporting this design choice.

For each candidate chunk and its contained candidate sentences, the chunk-level and sentence-level matching scores are computed as:
\begin{equation}
s_{c}
=
{\mathbf{q}^{(exp)}}^{T}
\mathbf{h}_{c}^{(final)}.
\label{eq:chunk_level_matching_score}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
s_{s}
=
{\mathbf{q}^{(exp)}}^{T}
\mathbf{h}_{s}^{(final)}.
\label{eq:sentence_level_matching_score}
\end{equation}
Here, $\mathbf{h}_{c}^{(final)}$ and $\mathbf{h}_{s}^{(final)}$ denote the final representations of the candidate chunk and candidate sentence, respectively.

For the entity-level score used in sentence reranking, we do not compress query entities into a single vector. Let $\mathbf{E}_{s}$ denote the entity set extracted from a candidate sentence $s$. The final representations of these entities form the context-aware candidate-sentence entity set
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{E}_{s}^{(final)}
=
\left\{
\mathbf{h}_{e_d}^{(final)}
\mid e_d \in \mathbf{E}_{s}
\right\}.
\label{eq:candidate_sentence_entity_set}
\end{equation}
We then calculate a MaxSim matching score between the final query entity set $\mathbf{E}_{q}^{(final)}$ and $\mathbf{E}_{s}^{(final)}$. Late-interaction retrieval models such as ColBERT show that retaining token- or entity-level matching signals can improve fine-grained semantic alignment while still enabling efficient retrieval \citep{Santhanam2022ColBERTv2}. Unlike the bottom-up aggregation stage in index construction, this score focuses on semantic alignment between context-aware entity representations. Therefore, it uses final entity representations rather than raw entity representations. The entity-level score is defined as:
\begin{equation}
s_{e}
=
\frac{1}{|\mathbf{E}_{q}^{(final)}|}
\sum_{e_q \in \mathbf{E}_{q}}
\max_{e_d \in \mathbf{E}_{s}}
\left(\mathbf{h}_{e_q}^{(final)}\right)^{T}
\mathbf{h}_{e_d}^{(final)}.
\label{eq:entity_level_matching_score}
\end{equation}
Here, $e_q$ and $e_d$ denote a query entity and an entity extracted from the candidate sentence, respectively; $\mathbf{h}_{e_q}^{(final)}$ and $\mathbf{h}_{e_d}^{(final)}$ are their corresponding final context-aware representations.

After obtaining the three granularity-specific scores for a candidate sentence, a temperature parameter $\tau'$ is introduced to adaptively generate granularity weights through a Softmax function:
\begin{equation}
[w_{c},w_{s},w_{e}]
=
\operatorname{Softmax}
\left(
\frac{[s_{c},s_{s},s_{e}]}{\tau'}
\right).
\end{equation}
The dynamically fused score used for sentence reranking is then computed as:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{S}^{(1)}
=
w_{c}s_{c}
+
w_{s}s_{s}
+
w_{e}s_{e}.
\end{equation}

According to this score, the Top-$p$ reranked sentences are selected as initial evidence. Multi-hop retrieval is then conducted by alternately traversing sentence and entity nodes. Starting from the selected sentences, their entities are used as lookup keys in a shared-entity inverted index to retrieve unvisited sentence nodes containing the same entities. The newly retrieved sentences are added as multi-hop evidence, and their entities are in turn used to retrieve further unvisited sentences. This sentence-to-entity-to-sentence expansion is repeated to connect support that may be distributed across different physical chunks. This design is motivated by multi-hop question answering settings in which answer evidence must be linked across supporting facts rather than retrieved as a single isolated passage \citep{Yang2018HotpotQA,Lan2023ComplexKBQA}.

Finally, the final answer is generated through a dual-track contrastive generation strategy. The chunk-level track consists of the top-ranked chunks retrieved by chunk-level semantic matching and establishes the topical scope, factual range, and necessary background. The sentence-level track comprises (i) directly matched and reranked sentences from within the retrieved chunks, which receive greater attention as fine-grained query-relevant candidates, and (ii) multi-hop candidate sentences obtained through shared-entity expansion. The retrieved chunks provide the contextual basis for interpreting and verifying the directly matched sentences. Multi-hop candidate sentences are incorporated only when they concern the target query entities and are consistent with the chunk-level context and directly matched sentences. When conflicts arise, conflicting multi-hop candidates are discarded, while the answer is grounded in the jointly supported chunk- and sentence-level evidence. This strategy is designed to prevent entity mismatching and unsupported associations introduced by loosely related multi-hop candidates during answer generation.

\section{Experiments}
\subsection{Experimental Setup}

We evaluate HoloHyper RAG using four research questions.

\textbf{RQ1: Answer quality.} Compared with existing RAG methods, can the proposed cascaded multi-granularity hypergraph framework improve the quality of generated answers in knowledge-intensive question answering?

\textbf{RQ2: Cross-domain generalization.} Can the proposed framework maintain stable and competitive performance across different domains?

\textbf{RQ3: Component effectiveness.} How much does each key component of the proposed framework contribute to the final performance?

\textbf{RQ4: Retrieval efficiency.} How does the proposed method's retrieval efficiency compare with existing RAG methods?

These questions are addressed through overall comparison, cross-domain evaluation, ablation study, and retrieval efficiency analysis.
\subsubsection{Datasets}

The evaluation uses eight document QA datasets, including five machine-tool subsets and three general-domain subsets. The machine-tool subsets, Mechanical, Hydraulics, Electrical, Maintenance, and System, are used as the primary industrial benchmark because their documents contain hierarchical technical descriptionsa and dense domain entities. The Mix,Pathology, and Physics subsets are used to test whether the retrieval strategy remains effective when terminology and document structure change. This cross-domain setting follows the broader QA evaluation practice of testing retrieval and generation methods beyond a single corpus or question type \citep{Rajpurkar2016SQuAD,Kwiatkowski2019NaturalQuestions,Yang2018HotpotQA}.

For each dataset, raw documents are deduplicated and converted into textual contexts. Questions are generated from the corresponding documents, and the associated reference contexts are retained as evidence for answer evaluation. The Mechanical subset further organizes questions into three nested complexity levels: single-stage questions requiring direct factual evidence, two-stage questions requiring one intermediate evidence connection, and three-stage questions requiring two connected evidence links. This design creates a controlled progression from direct answer extraction to increasingly compositional evidence requirements, conceptually aligning with staged query-complexity settings and multi-hop QA benchmarks. \subsubsection{Baselines}

We compare HoloHyper RAG with four baselines: LLM, RAG, LightRAG, and HyperRAG. LLM answers without retrieval, RAG represents dense retrieval-based RAG \citep{Karpukhin2020DPR,Lewis2020RAG}, LightRAG represents graph-enhanced RAG \citep{Guo2025LightRAG}, and HyperRAG is used as a hypergraph-based baseline \citep{Feng2026HyperRAG}. All retrieval-based methods use the same corpus, questions, embedding model, generation model, and evaluation protocol.

\subsubsection{Evaluation Metrics}

We adopt an LLM-as-a-judge protocol for open-ended knowledge-intensive question answering because strong LLM judges can approximate human preference judgments when prompts and protocols are controlled \citep{Zheng2023LLMJudge,Liu2023GEval}; RAG evaluation should also consider both answer quality and evidence use \citep{Es2024RAGAS}. Each answer is scored against its corresponding reference evidence on six criteria: Comprehensiveness, Diversity, Empowerment, Logical, Readability, and Relevance, using scores from 0 to 100. This protocol is used as the primary evaluation method because lexical or embedding-based metrics, such as BLEU, ROUGE, and BERTScore, focus on surface overlap or semantic similarity and may undervalue correct answers phrased differently from the reference \citep{Zhang2020BERTScore,Fabbri2021SummEval}. All compared methods use the same reference evidence, criteria, and judging prompt; the complete prompt and rubric are provided in Appendix~\ref{app:llm_judge_scoring_prompt}.

For each method, the score of a metric is computed by averaging the scores over all evaluated questions:
\begin{equation}
S_m^{(d)} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} s_{i,m}^{(d)},
\end{equation}
where \(s_{i,m}^{(d)}\) denotes the score of method \(m\) on the \(i\)-th question under metric \(d\), and \(N\) is the number of evaluated questions. The overall score is calculated as the average of the six metric scores:
\begin{equation}
S_m^{avg} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{D}|}\sum_{d \in \mathcal{D}} S_m^{(d)},
\end{equation}
where \(\mathcal{D}\) denotes the set of evaluation dimensions.

Thus, performance differences can be attributed to the ablated module itself rather than to changes in chunk segmentation or entity extraction. As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:retrieval_efficiency}, RAG has the lowest retrieval latency because it performs direct dense matching over text chunks. LightRAG also remains relatively efficient, while HyperRAG and HoloHyper RAG require more retrieval time because they perform structured retrieval over  hypergraph representations.

The efficiency result should be interpreted together with answer quality. HoloHyper RAG achieves the highest average score while keeping retrieval time comparable to the HyperRAG baseline. This indicates that topic-guided recall, multi-granularity sentence reranking, and multi-hop retrieval introduce structured retrieval cost, but they also improve evidence organization for knowledge-intensive questions.

\section{Discussion}

The overall comparison experiment results indicate that HoloHyper RAG provides a consistent advantage over competitive retrieval baselines. Its superiority is maintained for direct factual queries and for queries requiring one or two intermediate evidence connections. This pattern suggests that the proposed cascaded representation retains relationships among topics, chunks, sentences, and entities as evidence requirements become more complex. Compared with existing RAG baselines, HoloHyper RAG more effectively identifies and integrates complementary evidence distributed across different granularity levels. The consistent trend observed across different LLM backbones further suggests that the performance gain is mainly attributable to the retrieval architecture rather than the choice of generator alone.

The cross-domain evaluation provides evidence that HoloHyper RAG remains effective under a common evaluation protocol across eight datasets. HoloHyper RAG attains the highest average score on every dataset, spanning five machine-tool subsets and three general-domain subsets. Its advantage is also distributed across the six evaluation dimensions rather than being driven by a single criterion. Performance nevertheless varies across datasets, with all compared methods obtaining lower scores on Mix. This pattern suggests that heterogeneous terminology and evidence patterns make retrieval and evidence integration more challenging. Even under these conditions, HoloHyper RAG remains the top-performing method, indicating that its multi-granularity evidence organization transfers across the evaluated domains. These results support cross-domain applicability, while also showing that the absolute performance level is influenced by dataset characteristics.

The ablation results further clarify how the proposed components contribute to HoloHyper RAG. Removing either the cascaded multi-granularity hypergraph or query-side cascaded enhancement produced the largest performance losses, highlighting the importance of establishing a cascaded document representation and aligning the query with this representation. Disabling local attention fusion or the asymmetric bidirectional cascaded index also substantially reduced answer quality, indicating that multi-granularity retrieval benefits from combining local factual signals with higher-level contextual information. In addition, the reductions observed after removing dynamic multi-granularity scoring, multi-hop retrieval, or dual-track contrastive generation show that each module contributes to the overall performance of HoloHyper RAG.

The efficiency analysis places these quality gains in the context of online retrieval cost. Although HoloHyper RAG requires more retrieval time than dense RAG and LightRAG because it performs cascaded retrieval, multi-granularity reranking, and multi-hop retrieval, its retrieval latency remains comparable to that of HyperRAG. HoloHyper RAG therefore achieves improved answer quality without introducing a substantial additional latency cost relative to the structured hypergraph baseline. This result indicates a favourable quality–latency trade-off for knowledge-intensive question answering, particularly when reliable integration of distributed evidence is more important than minimizing retrieval time alone.

Several limitations remain. First, HoloHyper RAG depends on upstream chunking, topic construction, and entity extraction. Errors introduced at these stages can propagate through the cascaded index and affect subsequent retrieval. Second, constructing and maintaining the multi-granularity hypergraph requires additional offline processing and storage. Its scalability for substantially larger or frequently updated corpora therefore requires further evaluation. Third, multi-hop retrieval may introduce weakly related candidates when entities are frequent, increasing the difficulty of filtering distractors.

\section{Conclusion}

This paper proposed HoloHyper RAG based on the observation that documents comprise semantically organized units at multiple levels, including topics, chunks, sentences, and entities. Existing retrieval representations often do not explicitly preserve the organizational relationships among these levels. HoloHyper RAG addresses this limitation by modeling these relationships as a cascaded multi-granularity hypergraph and maintaining them throughout indexing, retrieval, and answer generation. This design enables retrieval to jointly exploit topic-level scope, contextual textual information, and entity-level factual evidence, thereby improving the identification and integration of relevant evidence for knowledge question answering. 

Experiments across LLM backbones, question stages, and domain settings demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed framework. HoloHyper RAG consistently achieves the strongest overall performance, particularly on questions requiring the identification and integration of distributed evidence. The ablation study further shows that each component of the multi-granularity hypergraph framework contributes to answer quality. The efficiency analysis shows that these gains are achieved with retrieval latency comparable to that of HyperRAG. These findings indicate that cascaded multi-granularity evidence organization provides an effective and practical basis for knowledge-intensive question answering.

Future work will focus on improving the robustness of upstream document processing, including topic construction, chunking, entity extraction, and entity normalization. It will also investigate adaptive, query-aware multi-hop expansion strategies to reduce weakly related candidates introduced by frequent or ambiguous entities. Finally, efficient incremental index updating and representation compression should be explored to support larger and continuously evolving corpora.

\section{Latent Topic Construction from Chunk Similarity}
\label{app:latent_topic_construction}

This appendix specifies how latent topics and their topic--chunk hyperedges are constructed. Let $\mathcal{C}=\{c_i\}_{i=1}^{N}$ denote the set of chunks, and let $\mathbf{x}_i\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ be the $L_2$-normalized raw embedding of chunk $c_i$. The pairwise semantic similarity between chunks is therefore computed by the dot product
\begin{equation}
s_{ij}=\mathbf{x}_i^{\top}\mathbf{x}_j,
\quad i\neq j.
\end{equation}
To retain only informative semantic connections, we compute a dynamic threshold from the upper-triangular similarity values:
\begin{equation}
\tau=\operatorname{mean}\left(\{s_{ij}\mid i<j\}\right)
+0.5\operatorname{std}\left(\{s_{ij}\mid i<j\}\right).
\end{equation}
We then construct a weighted undirected chunk-similarity graph $G=(\mathcal{C},E)$, where
\begin{equation}
(c_i,c_j)\in E\ \Longleftrightarrow\ s_{ij}>\tau,
\qquad w_{ij}=s_{ij}.
\end{equation}
Leiden community detection is applied to $G$ with edge weights $w_{ij}$ and resolution parameter $1.0$, producing a partition $\{C_k\}_{k=1}^{K}$ of the chunks \citep{Traag2019Leiden}. Each community defines one latent topic. Its topic-anchor representation is the normalized centroid of the member chunk embeddings:
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\mu}_k=\frac{1}{|C_k|}\sum_{c_i\in C_k}\mathbf{x}_i,
\qquad
\mathbf{t}_k=\frac{\boldsymbol{\mu}_k}{\|\boldsymbol{\mu}_k\|_2}.
\end{equation}

Although the Leiden partition provides a primary topic assignment, topic membership is made fuzzy to preserve cross-topic associations. For each chunk $c_i$ and topic anchor $\mathbf{t}_k$, we compute
\begin{equation}
m_{ik}=\mathbf{x}_i^{\top}\mathbf{t}_k.
\end{equation}
If $k$ is the primary Leiden community of $c_i$, we set $m_{ik}=1$. For every other topic with $m_{ik}>\eta$, where $\eta$ is a fuzzy-membership threshold, the chunk is assigned an additional membership with weight $m_{ik}$. The topic--chunk hyperedge associated with topic $k$ is thus
\begin{equation}
e_k^{T\rightarrow C}=\left\{c_i\in\mathcal{C}\mid m_{ik}>\eta\ \text{or}\ k=\operatorname{Leiden}(c_i)\right\}.
\end{equation}
Consequently, each hyperedge represents a latent topic as a set of semantically related chunks, while a chunk may participate in multiple topic hyperedges when it has sufficiently high similarity to multiple topic anchors.

\section{Diagnostic for Same-Entity Different-Sense Disambiguation Across Document Contexts}
\label{app:same_entity_different_sense_disambiguation}

To provide a targeted diagnostic for same-entity different-sense disambiguation across document contexts, we constructed a balanced set of 150 entity pairs from the fixed multi-domain corpus, comprising 75 same-sense pairs and 75 different-sense pairs. Each pair contains two occurrences of the same entity in different document contexts. Each occurrence was used only as a query-time context over the fixed corpus index. We treated different-sense pairs as positive instances and used cosine distance between the two contextualized representations as the disambiguation score. We report AUROC with 95\% confidence intervals obtained from 2,000 stratified bootstrap samples.

We compare a Raw condition, which uses only the static embedding of the target entity, with the Full cascade condition, which contextualizes the target using topic routing and retrieved chunk-, sentence-, and entity-level evidence. As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:same_entity_different_sense_disambiguation}, Raw matching cannot distinguish the two classes because identical entities receive identical static embeddings. In contrast, Full cascade achieves an AUROC of 0.774, with a 95\% confidence interval of $[0.691, 0.854]$. Its paired AUROC improvement over Raw is 0.274, with a 95\% bootstrap interval of $[0.191, 0.354]$. These results indicate that query-time cascaded contextualization can distinguish entities used with different meanings in the evaluated corpus.
